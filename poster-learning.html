01 The 4 Core Layers of an IoT System

1. Perception (Sensing)

Transducers convert physical phenomena into electrical signals. Analog sensors yield continuous voltage (0–3.3V/5V); digital sensors output binary states or serial telemetry packets.

2. Edge Processing

Onboard microcontrollers sample input signals, apply noise filtering/averaging algorithms, compute logic state machines, and prepare structured payloads at the device edge.

3. Connectivity & Buses

Local synchronous buses (I2C, SPI, UART) communicate with on-board modules. RF stacks (2.4GHz Wi-Fi, Bluetooth LE) stream real-time telemetry to dashboards and web servers.

4. Actuation & Action

Translates computational decisions into physical outcomes using relays (power switching), servos (angular position), buzzers (audio alarms), and OLED dashboards.

02 Core Microcontroller Comparison: Arduino Uno vs ESP32

Arduino Uno R3 — 5V Classic Controller

Architecture8-bit AVR ATmega328P @ 16 MHz Clock
Operating Voltage5V CMOS Logic — Extremely robust & noise-tolerant
Memory Capacity32 KB Flash, 2 KB SRAM, 1 KB EEPROM
ADC Precision10-bit Successive Approximation (1024 steps, 4.88 mV/LSB)
I/O Breakdown14 Digital I/O (6 PWM: 3, 5, 6, 9, 10, 11), 6 Analog Inputs (A0–A5)
Current LimitUp to 20 mA per I/O pin (safe limit), 500 mA total board draw
Best Suited ForDeterministic real-time control, basic sensor arrays, and beginner robotics

ESP32 Dev Module — 32-Bit Dual-Core IoT Hub

Architecture32-bit Xtensa Dual-Core LX6 @ up to 240 MHz Clock
Operating Voltage3.3V Logic — Strictly NOT 5V tolerant on GPIO inputs!
Memory Capacity4 MB SPI Flash, 520 KB SRAM, 448 KB ROM
ADC Precision12-bit SAR ADC (4096 steps, 0.8 mV resolution)
Wireless CommsIntegrated 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi (802.11 b/g/n) + Bluetooth v4.2 BR/EDR & BLE
Peripherals10 Capacitive Touch pins, 2 DAC channels, 3 UARTs, 2 I2C, 3 SPI
Best Suited ForIoT WebServers, cloud dashboards, REST APIs, and wireless telemetry
03 Essential Embedded Communication Buses

I2C Bus

  • 2-Wire Serial Bus: SDA (Data) & SCL (Clock)
  • Open-drain topology with pull-up resistors to VCC
  • Multi-device bus addressed by 7-bit ID (e.g. OLED = 0x3C)
  • Standard: 100 kHz; Fast Mode: 400 kHz
  • Use for: OLEDs, IMUs, RTCs, Barometers

SPI Bus

  • 4-Wire Synchronous: MOSI, MISO, SCK, SS/CS
  • Full-duplex synchronous transmission at high clock rates
  • Dedicated Chip Select (CS) controls active device
  • Blazing fast throughput up to 10–50 Mbps
  • Use for: RFID RC522, SD card readers, TFTs

UART Serial

  • 2 Lines: TX (Transmit) & RX (Receive) cross-wired
  • Point-to-point asynchronous packet framing
  • Standard Baud: 9600, 57600, 115200 bps
  • USB-UART bridge powers the Serial Monitor
  • Use for: GPS, Bluetooth modules, PC telemetry

PWM Power Driving

  • Simulates analog levels via rapid digital square waves
  • Duty Cycle: D = (T_on / T_period) × 100%
  • 50 Hz PWM (20ms frame) for Servos: 1ms=0°, 2ms=180°
  • 8-bit (0–255) on Uno; up to 16-bit LEDC on ESP32
  • Use for: LED dimming, motor speed, servos
04 Sensors & Transducers Reference

DHT11 Temp & Humidity

Capacitive humidity element + NTC thermistor. Outputs 40-bit custom single-wire digital packet with parity verification.

HC-SR04 Ultrasonic

Emits 40 kHz sonic bursts on trigger; measures echo return pulse width: d = (duration × 0.0343) / 2 cm.

MQ-Series Gas Sensors

Heated Tin Dioxide (SnO2) semiconductor. Conductivity increases proportionally with concentration of target gases.

LDR Photoresistor

Cadmium Sulfide (CdS) cell. Resistance drops exponentially from MΩ in darkness to a few hundred Ω under light.

PIR Motion Detector

Dual pyroelectric elements behind a Fresnel lens detect changes in radiated infrared heat from human motion.

Soil & Rain Probes

Conductive PCB traces where liquid bridges exposed paths, shifting analog resistance and measured ADC voltage.

Magnetic Reed Switch

Flexible ferromagnetic reeds sealed in glass attract and snap shut in the presence of an external magnetic field.

RC522 13.56 MHz RFID

Inductively powers passive 1KB tags via RF field and exchanges authentication & UID data over high-speed SPI.

05 Actuators & Power Electronics

5V Optocoupled Relay

  • Galvanic Isolation: Optocoupler protects MCU from AC/DC load spikes
  • Contacts: COM (Common), NO (Normally Open), NC (Normally Closed) up to 10A
  • Active-LOW: Digital LOW energizes optocoupler LED and triggers relay coil
  • Flyback Diode: Clamps high inductive reverse-voltage transients

SG90 Micro Servo Motor

  • Closed-Loop: DC motor, reduction gear train, and feedback potentiometer
  • PWM Timing (50 Hz / 20ms period):
    • 1.0 ms pulse = 0° (Minimum Position)
    • 1.5 ms pulse = 90° (Neutral / Center Position)
    • 2.0 ms pulse = 180° (Maximum Position)

Active Buzzer & Displays

  • Active Buzzer: Internal oscillator produces ~2.3 kHz acoustic tone on 5V DC
  • 0.96" SSD1306 OLED: 128x64 pixels, I2C, infinite contrast, 20mA power draw
  • 7-Segment Display: Multiplexed LEDs in Common Cathode format
  • RGB LEDs: Tri-color additive mixing via PWM duty cycles
06 Golden Rules of Circuit Prototyping & Safety

Common Ground (GND)

Always tie GND pins of all power supplies, sensors, and controllers together to guarantee a common zero-volt reference.

3.3V vs 5V Logic

ESP32 pins are strictly 3.3V and NOT 5V tolerant. Always use a 10k/20k voltage divider when reading 5V sensor signals.

Inductive Protection

Pumps and motors generate high reverse-voltage spikes upon power shutoff; always drive through buffered relays with flyback diodes.

Input-Only Pins & Noise

ESP32 GPIO 34, 35, 36, and 39 are input-only without internal pull-ups. Apply a 12-sample averaging filter to suppress ADC noise.
